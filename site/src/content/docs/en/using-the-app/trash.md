What you delete in a library does not go at once: it goes to that library's **trash** and stays there for **30 days**. While it is there, it can be **restored** and it comes back as it was. After that, it is deleted for good.

Each library has its own trash. It is in the menu, with the library's things: "Trash".

## What goes to the trash and what does not

The four things you delete from the app go to the trash:

- **A work** (a book, a game, a film…), with all its copies.
- **A single copy.** The work stays in the catalog.
- **A tag.** The works that had it are not deleted.
- **A saga or collection.** Its works are not deleted either: only the grouping goes.

While something is in the trash, the app acts as if it were not there: it does not show in the catalog, in searches, in "I have it", in "Missing", in the counts of sagas, or when exporting.

**These do not go to the trash:**

- **Taking a work out of a saga**, or removing a "needs": they undo a link, they delete nothing, and they are redone in two taps.
- **People and devices.** For that there is [Remove a person or a device](../remove-someone/).
- **A whole library.** "Delete library" deletes it for good, with its trash, and first offers to save a backup. It is in [Several libraries](../several-libraries/).

## Who sees it

Whoever **edits** or **administers** the library sees its trash and **restores** anything in it, whoever deleted it.

Deleting, that is, sending something to the trash, works as before: whoever administers deletes anything, and whoever edits, what that same person added; anything else, they ask for. Whoever only **reads** does not see the trash: when they ask for something to be deleted and it is accepted, it goes to the trash all the same. The roles are in [People and permissions](../people-and-permissions/).

## Undo at once

When you delete, a notice at the bottom says "Moved to the trash · Undo". **Undo** restores it at once, as if nothing had happened.

## Restore

In the trash, each thing says what it is, who deleted it, when, and how many days it has left ("Goes for good in 12 days").

- **One:** tap "Restore" on its row.
- **Several at once:** on a computer, tick their boxes; on a phone, **press and hold** one, then tap the others. A bar at the top shows how many you have picked, "Select all" and the buttons. The back button leaves the selection without doing anything.

What is restored comes back **with everything it had**: a work with its copies, its authors, its genres and tags, each person's marks (read, stars…) and its place in sagas. A tag comes back on all its works, and a saga with all of its works and their numbers.

If you deleted a single copy and then the whole work, restoring the work leaves that copy in the trash: it is restored on its own.

## Delete for good

**Whoever administers** can delete something from the trash without waiting: "Delete for good", on its row or in the selection bar. The app asks first, because **it cannot be undone**: it goes from every device of the library.

Whoever edits does not have that button. Their things go by themselves after 30 days.

## When it goes for good

- **After 30 days**, the first device of the library that opens or syncs, of someone who edits or administers, deletes it for everyone.
- Each device counts the 30 days from when **it** first saw that thing in the trash. So if another device's clock is wrong, nothing is deleted early.
- **The cloud**, if you use one, is the safety net: what has been **60 days** in the trash, it deletes itself, in case no device connects in all that time. The cloud cannot do anything else with your catalog.

If one person restores something just as another deletes it, deleting wins.

## If you add something that is in the trash

So that you do not end up with two alike, the app tells you when what you are adding is already in the trash:

- **A work:** when you scan its code or pick it in the "Add" search, it says "In the trash".
- **A tag:** when you type its name on a work, instead of "Create" it says "Restore from the trash".
- **A saga or collection:** when you create one with the same name.

The app says what comes back with it ("it comes back with its 2 copies", "with its 12 works") and offers to **restore it**. If you would rather start over, whoever administers can **delete the one in the trash and create another**. Whoever edits can only restore or cancel: to start over, they have to ask whoever administers to delete it.

In "Quick add" and in the shelf photo, what is in the trash is not added: it is marked, and restored from the trash.

## What may come back different

- **What was deleted for good in the meantime.** If a tag or a saga was deleted for good while its work was in the trash, the work comes back without it.
- **What was changed from another device.** If someone changed that work offline, before learning it was in the trash, their change is not lost: the work stays in the trash and, when restored, comes back with that change.

## Moving to another library

Moving a thing to another library creates it in the new one and leaves it **in the trash of the old one**. If you picked the wrong library, it is there for 30 days. It is in [Several libraries](../several-libraries/).

## Backups

A [backup](../backups/) also keeps the trash. When you restore it, what it brings in the trash gets **30 more days** from that moment, even if the backup is months old. The summary says so: "It brings 5 things in the trash: you have 30 days to restore them".

That is why the trash does not replace backups: after 30 days, what was deleted only comes back with an older backup.

## Every device on the same version

The trash needs **every device of the library** to have the version of the app that brings it. One with an older version would not know what to do with what is in it, so update them all.
